# ADR: Club memberships (EU, EEA, eurozone, customs union)

Status: accepted for v1.12.0 Phase 1 (snapshots only; no wrapper or CLI change)

## Context
`is_eu`, `is_eea` and `is_eurozone` are heuristics today. They cannot say that
GB was in the EU until 2020-01-31 or that GR joined the eurozone on 2001-01-01.
ISO 3166 does not publish club membership, so this is a fact ISO does not carry.
The introduced-field ADR allows Wikipedia as an explicit exception for such facts.

## Source
Wikipedia, English edition, read 2026-10-29:
- Member state of the European Union (EU)
- European Economic Area (EEA)
- Eurozone / Euro area (eurozone). Cross-checked against the Council of the EU page "Timeline - Joining the euro area". That cross-check is a second source, not the source of record.
- European Union Customs Union (customs union)

Permalink status: the research tool returned page text without revision ids,
so `source_permalink` is the canonical title URL, not an `oldid` URL. This is a
known deviation from the introduced snapshot. Pin an `oldid` at the first review.

## Files
`tools/{eu_membership,eea_membership,eurozone_membership,customs_union}_snapshot.json`,
each with a sibling `*.meta.json` (same pattern as `introduced_snapshot`),
generated by `tools/build_club_snapshots.py`.

## Shape and semantics
`{"meta": {...}, "data": {"<alpha_2>": [{"from": "YYYY-MM-DD", "to": "YYYY-MM-DD" | null}, ...]}}`
- `from` is the first day of membership. `to` is the last day of membership, inclusive. `null` means current.
- A country re-joining gets a second range. Ranges are sorted and must not overlap. The generator asserts this.
- Keys are ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 codes of active entries. Greece is `GR`, not `EL`. The generator asserts every key exists in `iso3166.json`.

## Null policy
A country that is not in a club has no key. There is no null array and no empty array.

## Coverage (2026-10-29)
| Club | Countries | Notes |

## Decisions that need a maintainer's eye
1. EEA `from` uses the formal entry-into-force date (2005-12-06 for the 2004 ten, 2011-11-09 for BG/RO, 2025-02-19 for HR), as in Wikipedia's table. The EEA enlargement was applied provisionally earlier (2004-05-01 and 2007-08-01; Wikipedia gives no HR date). The provisional dates are in `meta.provisional_application`, not in the ranges. A consumer asking "was BG in the EEA in 2010?" gets false. Reverse this if you want effective-application semantics.
2. EEC founders (BE, DE, FR, IT, LU, NL): the Wikipedia table says "Founder" with no date. 1958-01-01 (EEC in force) is used. [Likely] Alternatives: 1952-07-23 (ECSC), 1957-03-25 (signature).
3. GB EEA and customs union end 2020-12-31 (transition end), not 2020-01-31. Wikipedia's EEA state table says "31 January 2020" while its prose and the UK-EEA article say 31 December 2020. [Likely]
4. Customs union start for the founders is 1968-07-01. Monaco's date is year-only in the source; 1968-07-01 is an alignment, flagged [Guess] in `meta.imprecise_dates`.
5. The eurozone 1999-01-01 date is the launch of the euro as book-entry money. Cash came 2002-01-01.

## Not modelled
Gibraltar (provisional customs union from 2026-07-15, not in force), Northern Ireland
under the Windsor Framework, Akrotiri and Dhekelia (no ISO code), sub-national carve-outs
(Büsingen, Heligoland, Livigno, Ceuta, Melilla), euro users outside the eurozone
(AD, MC, SM, VA, XK, ME), and partial scope (AD and TR exclude agricultural produce;
recorded in `meta.partial_scope`).

## Refresh cadence
Annual. `review_by` is 2027-10-29 in every snapshot, checked by `tools/check_snapshot_freshness.py`.
Re-run the generator with updated tables, then `python3 tools/build_club_snapshots.py --check`.
Do an out-of-cycle refresh on any accession, withdrawal, euro adoption or EEA enlargement.
Known next event: none scheduled in source as of the access date.

## Consequences
Phase 2 can replace the heuristics with date-scoped lookups. A wrong date here is a wrong answer
there, so decisions 1 to 4 should be settled before Phase 2 starts.

## Consumption (v1.12.0, Phase 2)
`tools/enrich_club_memberships.py` copies each snapshot's ranges onto the active entries of
`iso3166.json` as `eu_membership`, `eea_membership`, `eurozone_membership` and `customs_union`.
Codes absent from a snapshot get `[]`. Withdrawn entries carry none of the four. The snapshots stay
the source of truth: `--check` fails if the tree drifts from them, and it runs in the release gate and in CI.
The wrappers and CLIs read the arrays (docs/QUERIES.md, Query 10); `is_eu`, `is_eea` and `is_eurozone`
are current-status aliases over the same arrays.

Refresh: edit the tables in `tools/build_club_snapshots.py`, then run it, then
`python3 tools/enrich_club_memberships.py --write`, `python3 tools/sync_wrappers.py` and
`python3 tools/gen_consistency_fixture.py`.

## Flat exports (v1.12.1)
The CSV, TSV, SQL and Parquet exports carry the four arrays as four trailing string columns
(`eu_membership`, `eea_membership`, `eurozone_membership`, `customs_union`). One range is
`from:to`, an empty `to` means still a member, several ranges are joined with `|` (the delimiter the
exports already use for lists): `1973-01-01:2020-01-31`, `2004-05-01:`. A country that was never a
member has an empty cell (CSV, TSV), `NULL` (SQL, Parquet); so does every withdrawn entry. The
serializer is `tools/club_format.py`; the packaged CLI has a byte-identical copy so that its `--csv`
and `--tsv` output still equals the root files.
